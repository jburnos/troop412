The Linked Troop Advantage

One Family Drop-Off. Two Independent Programs.

Troop 412 (for young men) and Troop 6412 (for young women) operate under a linked charter model at Olathe Christian Church. Both troops maintain separate youth officers and patrols while sharing logistics, equipment, and meeting times.

Troop 412 (Boys)

Chartered by Olathe Christian Church

Ages 11–17

Our program for young men emphasizes patrol autonomy, outdoor self-reliance, and youth mentoring.

  • Youth Leadership: Senior Patrol Leader (SPL) and Patrol Leaders run all weekly troop meetings.
  • Patrol Structure: Scouts plan menus, cook patrol-style meals, and manage duty rosters.
  • Meeting Night: Tuesdays, 7:00 PM – 8:30 PM at Olathe Christian Church.
  • Advancement: Active Eagle Scout mentoring pipeline with dedicated adult coaches.

Troop 6412 (Girls)

Chartered by Olathe Christian Church

Ages 11–17

Our program for young women provides full outdoor challenge, wilderness leadership, and camaraderie.

  • Youth Leadership: Dedicated female youth SPL and Patrol Leaders elected by the girls.
  • Patrol Structure: Independent girl patrols directing campcraft, pioneering, and treks.
  • Meeting Night: Tuesdays, 7:00 PM – 8:30 PM at Olathe Christian Church.
  • Advancement: Proven path to Scouting BSA's highest rank of Eagle Scout.

Why Olathe Families Love Linked Scouting

Instead of shuttling children to multiple locations on different nights, families with both sons and daughters enjoy a single drop-off with matched calendars.

Unified Drop-off & Pick-up

Both troops gather simultaneously every Tuesday at 7:00 PM at Olathe Christian Church. Parents make one trip.

Shared Equipment Locker

Combined access to our heavy gear trailer: canvas patrol dining flies, cast iron Dutch ovens, Coleman stoves, and canoes.

Joint High Adventure Treks

Coordinated trips to Philmont, Sea Base, Northern Tier, and the H. Roe Bartle Scout Reservation reduce per-family costs.

Parent Fact Sheet & Enrollment

Everything You Need to Know About Joining

Whether your child has been a Cub Scout for years or has never pitched a tent before, Troops 412 and 6412 welcome all youth ages 11–17 to begin their adventure.

We operate as linked troops. Troop 412 (boys) and Troop 6412 (girls) maintain their own separate youth leadership, patrols, and rank advancement structures. However, we meet on the exact same night at Olathe Christian Church, coordinate our camping calendar, and share committee oversight. Parents enjoy "one drop-off, two amazing programs."
We camp once a month, 12 months a year! In accordance with our Strategic Framework, monthly camping is our top program priority. Outings range from local state parks (Clinton Lake, HaHa Tonka) to high adventures like the Strataca Underground Salt Mine, fly fishing, and our 4-week window at the H. Roe Bartle Scout Reservation in June.
In Scouting BSA, adults provide two-deep safety, mentorship, and transportation—but the Scouts run the show! The Patrol Leaders' Council (PLC), guided by the Senior Patrol Leader (SPL), decides the meeting agendas, chooses campout themes, builds duty rosters, and teaches junior scouts outdoor skills.
Troop meetings are held every Tuesday from 7:00 PM – 8:30 PM (1.5 hours), alongside one weekend campout per month. Scouting is intentionally flexible: we have multi-sport athletes, band members, and scholars who excel in our troops. Scouts advance at their own personal pace.
Very little! The troop provides all heavy equipment: tents, dining canopies, propane stoves, Dutch ovens, and patrol cooking boxes. New scouts only need a sleeping bag, sleeping pad, water bottle, pocket knife (after earning their Totin' Chip), and a field uniform. We also maintain a troop gear loaner locker so financial constraints never prevent a scout from camping.

4 Simple Steps to Join

1

Visit a Tuesday Meeting

Join us any Tuesday at 7:00 PM at Olathe Christian Church. Meet our Scoutmasters, talk with youth patrol leaders, and see the program in action.

2

Attend an Open House or Campout

Bring your scout to an open house, pinewood derby, or our annual "Bring-a-Buddy" campout to get their hands on outdoor skills.

3

Complete Official Registration

Submit your Scouting BSA youth registration. Both Troop 412 (Boys) and Troop 6412 (Girls) are chartered by Olathe Christian Church.

4

Join Your Patrol & Hit the Trail

Receive your custom troop neckerchief, get assigned to a patrol of your peers, and start your path toward Eagle Scout!

Leadership Governance

2027 Strategic Framework & Hierarchy of Priorities

Adopted by the Troop Committee and Patrol Leaders' Council to guide our operational decisions, calendar development, and financial investments through 2027.

The 5 Core Strategic Pillars

Pillar 1

Outdoor Camping & Adventure First

Monthly troop camping serves as our foundational activity, preceding meetings, Eagle projects, and service hours. Active High and Low adventure planning.

Pillar 2

Youth-Led & Patrol-Driven

Develop youth leadership roles across both troops. Active coaching for Patrol Leaders (PLs) and Troop Guides with patrol-style cooking and camping.

Pillar 3

Continuous Year-Round Recruiting

Consistent youth membership growth across Troop 412 (boys) and Troop 6412 (girls). Monthly recruitment events with open-door policy at all meetings.

Pillar 4

Financial Self-Reliance

Prioritize unit fundraising and revenue opportunities to upgrade camping equipment and subsidize High & Low Adventure treks for every Scout.

Pillar 5

Honor Camping & Venture Synergy

Support H. Roe Bartle staff and honor camping programs. Construct annual calendar around tradition and partner closely with the Venture Crew.

2027 Hierarchy of Scheduling & Financial Priorities

When making troop scheduling, budget, and resource decisions, leadership adheres strictly to this rank order:

Priority 1
Troop / Unit First

Strengthening primary troop operations, safety, health, and member retention of Troops 412 and 6412.

Priority 2
Patrol Activities & Outdoor Leadership

Fostering patrol identity, patrol-style cooking, outdoor skill mastery, and Patrol Leaders' Council authority.

Priority 3
Service to Others

Executing Eagle Scout service projects, monthly community service hours, and local food drive initiatives.

Priority 4
Service to God

Supporting duty to God, spiritual growth, and individual Scout faith commitments at meetings and campouts.

Priority 5
Honor Camping Service

Honoring traditions and serving through recognized Honor Camping programs, OA inductions, and Bartle staff opportunities.
